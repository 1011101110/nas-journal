## Infant (1 – 12 months)

**What defines this stage:** The acute withdrawal phase is over. What remains is a nervous system that calibrated differently — and will now interact with every developmental task from that altered baseline.

---

### What's Happening Neurologically

By month 1–2, the acute NAS crisis has passed. What remains:

- **Altered autonomic nervous system function:** Lower vagal tone, slower recovery from stress activation, higher baseline arousal
- **Sensory hypersensitivity:** Sensory gating (the brain's ability to suppress background input) is less efficient. Ordinary environments are more stimulating.
- **Disrupted attachment foundation:** The stress of NAS, NICU separation, and caregiver stress can complicate early bonding. Attachment is not determined in the first weeks, but the foundation is being laid.

The good news: the infant brain is maximally plastic. The experiences of this year — relational, sensory, environmental — directly shape the nervous system. This is both the vulnerability and the opportunity.

---

### What to Expect This Year

**Sleep (ongoing challenge):**
- Fragmented sleep cycles, difficulty settling, frequent night waking through 6–9 months
- Consistent bedtime routine is the most effective structural intervention ([Sleep](/symptoms/sleep))
- Night waking reducing by 9–12 months is realistic with consistent routine; earlier resolution is less common in NAS

**Sensory sensitivity (peaks 3–6 months):**
- Auditory, tactile, and visual hypersensitivity most prominent
- Certain textures, sounds, or light levels will trigger crying that looks inconsolable
- Environmental modification (low light, white noise, consistent tactile inputs) reduces frequency ([Sensory Overload](/symptoms/sensory-overload))

**Feeding:**
- Oral-motor difficulties often persist from NICU phase
- Texture sensitivity beginning around solid introduction (4–6 months)
- Feeding therapy assessment worth considering if solid introduction is significantly difficult ([Feeding](/symptoms/feeding))

**Crying and screaming:**
- High-pitched cry typically decreases by 3–4 months but may persist in modified form
- Inconsolable periods become less frequent as vagal tone matures ([Screaming](/symptoms/screaming))

### Relationship-Building Is Clinical Work

Secure attachment is the most important buffer against the long-term effects of early adversity. For POE kids specifically, a secure attachment relationship with the primary caregiver predicts better behavioral outcomes at age 3–5.

This means caregiver sensitivity — responding consistently to the infant's cues — matters more in this year than almost anything else. Responsive caregiving is not spoiling. It is building the relational template that the stress response system will learn to use.

**What responsive caregiving looks like:**
- Respond to distress cues promptly (not perfectly, promptly)
- Follow the child's attention lead in play
- Match affect — mirror positive states, soothe negative ones
- Consistent caregiver face — don't present a blank or distressed face repeatedly

**Caregiver mental health is part of this.** Parental depression and anxiety directly impair the responsiveness that builds attachment. If you're struggling, that matters clinically — not just personally.

---

### Early Intervention

If you're not already connected to early intervention services, pursue them now. Most states provide free services from birth to age 3 for children with known developmental risk factors (NAS qualifies in most states).

Referrals to pursue:
- **Developmental pediatrician** — comprehensive developmental assessment
- **Early intervention** (Birth to 3 program) — OT, PT, speech evaluation at no cost
- **Infant mental health therapist** — for parent-infant relationship support
- **Feeding therapy** — if feeding is significantly difficult

Getting on waitlists now matters — these lists are long.

---

### What Comes Next

The big behavioral challenges — tantrums, transitions, sensory meltdowns, sleep defiance — are primarily toddler phenomena. The infant year is about nervous system stabilization, relationship-building, and building the early intervention team you'll need.
