## Theraplay

**What it is:** A structured, evidence-based therapy developed by Ann Jernberg in the late 1960s that works directly on the parent-child relationship through play. Unlike most behavioral therapies, Theraplay doesn't target behaviors directly — it targets the relational substrate that regulates behavior.  
**Time horizon:** Months of weekly sessions  
**Priority:** Core strategy  
**Status:** Just beginning — this page will be updated as learnings accumulate.

---

### The Mechanism

Children with prenatal opioid exposure have stress response systems shaped by a dysregulated prenatal environment and, often, early relational disruption. The combination produces deficits in felt safety, trust in caregiver responsiveness, and the capacity to co-regulate through relationship.

Theraplay works on exactly this substrate.

Four dimensions organize all Theraplay activities:

- **Structure** — the therapist (and caregiver) provide clear, safe structure, reducing the child's need to control the environment to feel safe
- **Engagement** — joyful, direct interaction that builds connection and the expectation of positive relational experience
- **Nurture** — physical care, soothing, and attunement that build the child's experience of being cared for
- **Challenge** — age-appropriate activities that build competence and trust within the relationship

For a POE child — whose nervous system learned to expect unpredictability, whose early relational experiences may have included disruption, who may not have built a secure attachment template — these four dimensions address the actual gaps.

---

### How It Differs from PCIT

**PCIT (Parent-Child Interaction Therapy)** is parent-coached: a therapist coaches the parent through an earpiece while the parent interacts with the child. The therapist teaches the parent skills that the parent then applies at home. PCIT's mechanism is primarily behavioral — building regulatory capacity through the consistent parent interaction pattern.

**Theraplay** is therapist-led: the therapist works directly with the child in session, often with the parent present as observer and then active participant. The therapist models attunement and co-regulation directly. The mechanism is explicitly relational — building the child's internal model of safe relationship through repeated experience.

Both are well-supported. They address overlapping but distinct aspects of the POE deficit profile.

For a child with early relational disruption (foster care, NICU separation, early attachment uncertainty), Theraplay's explicit focus on the relational experience may address something PCIT's behavioral structure does not.

---

### What It Addresses

- Attachment and felt safety
- Emotional regulation through co-regulation
- Sensory tolerance within a relational context
- Transition difficulty (structure and predictability are built into the model)
- Parent-child connection
- Trust in caregiver responsiveness

---

### How to Find a Theraplay Therapist

- The Theraplay Institute maintains a directory of certified therapists: [theraplay.org](https://theraplay.org)
- Ask specifically about certification level — Theraplay has a formal certification pathway
- Ask about experience with POE or early relational trauma — not all Theraplay therapists have this specific background
- Developmental pediatrics programs at children's hospitals may be able to refer you to Theraplay-trained therapists in your area
- Early intervention coordinators are often a good referral source
