Every parent watches the milestone charts. NAS parents watch them with a particular kind of dread.

Here's what the research actually shows: many NAS babies hit milestones within normal ranges. Some have delays, particularly in motor development and language. Early intervention makes a measurable difference.

What I tracked:
- Rolling, sitting, pulling up — roughly on schedule for him
- Fine motor (pincer grasp, stacking) — slightly delayed, addressed with OT
- Language — first words on the later end of normal; speech therapy at 15 months

How to access early intervention:
1. Ask your pediatrician for a referral, or
2. Contact your state's early intervention program directly (every state has one, covered under IDEA, federally mandated for ages 0-3)
3. You don't need a diagnosis — developmental concern is enough

**Don't wait to see if they catch up.** The research on early intervention is clear: earlier is better. The 0-3 window is when the brain is most plastic. Services during this period have outsized effects.

You are your child's advocate. If you think something's off, push for the evaluation. You know your kid.
