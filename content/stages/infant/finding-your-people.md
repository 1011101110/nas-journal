Around month four, I realized I hadn't told most of my friends what was actually going on. I'd said "NICU stay" and "he had some challenges at birth" and let them fill in the blanks with prematurity or heart issues — anything but the truth.

The shame wasn't mine to carry. But it took a long time to understand that.

NAS parenting is isolating in a specific way. The stigma around opioid use affects children too — people make assumptions, ask invasive questions, offer unsolicited opinions. So you learn to be careful about who you tell.

But isolation is its own damage. You need people who understand, who don't flinch, who've been up at 2 AM with the same kind of cry.

Where we found community:
- **Online groups** — search "NAS parents support group" on Facebook; they're active and judgment-free
- **The NICU social worker** — ours connected us to a local family support program
- **Early intervention programs** — not just for the baby; the coordinators often know other NAS families
- **Hospital family support programs** — many hospitals with NICUs have follow-up programs specifically for NAS families

You don't have to tell everyone everything. But you need someone who knows.
