title: "Sensory Sensitivities"
description: "Why everything feels louder, brighter, and more overwhelming for NAS babies."
stage: infant
order: 2
---

At his six-month well visit, I tried to describe it to the pediatrician. "He just seems... overwhelmed? By things that shouldn't be overwhelming?"

She nodded. She'd seen this before.

NAS affects the central nervous system — specifically, the ability to regulate sensory input. Opioids during development affect neurotransmitter systems that are still being built. The result is often a nervous system that's hypervigilant, that treats too much as threatening.

For us, this looked like:
- Crying when we took him from a quiet room to a louder one
- Arching away from certain textures (rough fabric, grass)
- Inconsolable periods that seemed to come from nowhere
- Extreme distress during diaper changes — the temperature change, the touch, the position shift

What we learned:
- **Warn him.** Talk through what's about to happen before it happens. Even at 6 months, the voice mattered.
- **Slow down transitions.** Don't just pick him up — let him see you first, then touch, then lift.
- **Watch for "tells."** Every baby has signs they're approaching overload. We learned his.
- **Less is more.** Fewer toys, quieter environment, shorter outings at first.

The sensory piece gets better with age and support. Most NAS kids aren't still overwhelmed by grass at age 5. But the early months require real accommodation.
