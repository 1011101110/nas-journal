By month three, I had stopped counting sleep hours because the number was too discouraging. NAS babies are often light sleepers, easily startled, and unable to self-soothe the way sleep training books assume.

The startle reflex (Moro reflex) that fades in typical babies by 3-4 months often persists longer in NAS babies. A sound, a shift in light, a change in air pressure — and he'd jerk awake, arms flailing, and we'd be back at square one.

What eventually worked for us:
- **Tight swaddle until he couldn't be swaddled anymore** — this is longer than most parents need
- **White noise at the highest safe volume** — not gentle ocean sounds, full static
- **Contact naps in those early months** — I know everyone says don't, but survival required it
- **Gradual transition to crib** — starting with naps, then first stretch of night, slowly extending

The hardest part was accepting that the sleep books weren't written for us. I had to let go of what "should" happen and just work with the baby in front of me.

Your exhaustion is real. Protect yourself too. Take shifts with your partner. Accept help. This phase does end.
