The day we left the NICU, I cried in the parking garage. Not from happiness — from fear. For 22 days, there had been monitors and nurses and a call button. Now it was just us, and a baby whose nervous system was still figuring itself out.

The NICU gave us a discharge sheet. It said to watch for high-pitched crying, tremors, poor feeding, excessive sweating. It did not say how to tell normal newborn fussiness from NAS symptoms. It did not say what to do at 2 AM when you can't tell the difference.

Here's what I wish someone had told me:

**The Finnegan doesn't matter anymore.** At home, you're not scoring symptoms — you're just responding to your baby. Trust your instincts.

**Keep the environment calm.** NICU rules still apply at home. Dim lights, low noise, minimal visitors for the first few weeks. Some well-meaning relatives will not understand this.

**The pediatrician needs to know.** Find one who has experience with NAS babies before you're discharged. The NICU social worker can help with referrals.

**You're going to be okay.** Both of you.
