The first thing I noticed was how different his cry sounded. Not the soft mewing of a newborn getting used to the world — it was sharp, high-pitched, urgent. The nurses explained it was the NAS cry, and once you hear it, you never forget it.

He was in the NICU for 22 days. I learned to read the Finnegan scores like I'd been doing it my whole life. 8 was the threshold. We watched that number like a stock ticker.

What helped in those first days: being there. Holding him skin-to-skin whenever they'd let me. The nurses taught me to swaddle tight — tighter than felt comfortable to me — because the containment helped calm his nervous system. Dark room, low voices, minimal stimulation.

Nobody tells you how quiet the NICU needs to be for these babies. Regular newborns can handle the bustle. NAS babies are already overwhelmed just being alive right now. Every sound, every light, every touch is amplified in a nervous system that never had a chance to calibrate normally.

I didn't know any of this before. I'm writing it down so maybe you don't have to learn it the hard way.
