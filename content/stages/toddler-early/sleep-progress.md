title: "Sleep Progress (Finally)"
description: "How sleep slowly improved and what we did to help it along."
stage: toddler-early

---

Around 14 months, sleep started to consolidate. Not all at once — there were regressions with each new developmental leap, and a brutal stretch around 15 months when he was working on walking — but the trend was finally upward.

What had changed by this point:
- The Moro startle reflex had finally faded
- He'd developed enough language to start understanding "it's okay, you can sleep"
- We'd established a ritual that was unbreakable in its consistency

Our bedtime routine (30 minutes, every night, no exceptions):
1. Bath (warm water, same temperature, minimal toys)
2. Lotion massage (he likes deep pressure — firm strokes, not light touches)
3. Pajamas and sleep sack
4. One book, lights dim
5. White noise on, same song hummed
6. Put down awake but drowsy

The consistency was everything. NAS kids crave predictability. When they can predict what comes next, their nervous systems can relax.

We still used white noise at high volume. We still had the room very dark. The sensory accommodations don't disappear — they just become normal.

I've stopped apologizing for having a strict bedtime routine. It works. That's enough.
