At 15 months, he had maybe 5 words. The guideline is 10-15 by 12 months, and several emerging phrases by 18 months. We weren't on track.

I'd been watching it and hoping. Our pediatrician, bless her, finally said: "Let's not wait." She referred us to early intervention for a speech evaluation.

The evaluation took about 90 minutes. The speech-language pathologist played with him, watched how he communicated, noted his comprehension versus his expression. Her assessment: receptive language was mostly on track (he understood what we said) but expressive language was delayed.

Six months of speech therapy, twice a week. By 18 months, he'd exploded into 30+ words. By 24 months, two-word phrases.

What I learned:
- **Comprehension and expression are separate** — a child who understands everything but doesn't speak much is different from a child who doesn't seem to understand either
- **Sign language helps** — we used basic signs to bridge the gap; it didn't delay speech, it supported it
- **Modeling, not quizzing** — speech therapy taught me to narrate and model, not to ask "what's that?" constantly
- **Read aloud constantly** — the correlation between reading aloud and language development is as strong as anything in developmental science

If you're worried about speech, get the evaluation. Early intervention is free under age 3. There's no downside to getting information.
