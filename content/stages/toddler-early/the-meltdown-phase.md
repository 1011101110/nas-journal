Every toddler melts down. NAS toddlers melt down differently.

The intensity is different. The duration is different. The recovery time is different. And for a while, I thought I was doing something wrong as a parent.

I wasn't. The dysregulation is neurological, not behavioral in origin. A typical toddler's prefrontal cortex is offline during a tantrum. A NAS toddler's nervous system has been running in a higher-alert state since before birth. The meltdowns are bigger because the baseline is different.

What this looked like for us at 12-15 months:
- Transitions (even good ones — leaving the park) could trigger 20-minute meltdowns
- Hunger made everything 10x worse, faster than typical
- Once started, the meltdowns had to complete — you couldn't interrupt them
- Physical comfort helped but only once he was past the peak

What helped:
- **Anticipating triggers** and modifying environment when possible
- **Countdowns for transitions** — "5 more minutes, then we go"
- **Consistent routines** — predictability is calming when your nervous system is unpredictable
- **Not fighting the meltdown** — staying calm, staying near, letting it move through

We found a developmental pediatrician who had NAS experience. That changed everything. She gave us language for what was happening that helped us explain it to daycare, to family, to ourselves.
