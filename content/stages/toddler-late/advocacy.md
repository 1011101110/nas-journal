By now, if you're in this journey, you've probably encountered systems — medical, educational, insurance, developmental. Some of them are helpful. Many require pushing.

Things I've learned about advocacy:

**Document everything.** Dates of evaluations, names of providers, what was said, what was recommended, what insurance approved or denied. Keep a folder. You'll refer to it.

**Ask for things in writing.** Verbal recommendations from doctors are nice. Written referrals, letters of medical necessity, evaluation reports — these are the currency of the systems you're navigating.

**Know your rights.** Under IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act), your child is entitled to free early intervention services from birth to age 3, and free appropriate public education from age 3. This includes evaluations. It includes services. School districts cannot charge you.

**IEP vs 504.** An Individualized Education Program provides specialized instruction and services. A 504 Plan provides accommodations within general education. Both are federal rights. Neither requires a severe disability — a functional impairment that affects education is enough.

**You can ask for a meeting.** Anytime. In writing. Schools are required to respond.

**Get a second opinion.** If a provider dismisses your concerns, find another provider. You know your child. Medical providers have limited time and sometimes limited experience with NAS specifically.

You are not a difficult parent. You are an informed one. There's a difference.
