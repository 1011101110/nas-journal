As we approached three years old, the question of preschool started looming. Most typical children transition to preschool settings with some adjustment. For NAS kids, the sensory environment, transition demands, and group dynamics can be a bigger leap.

We started preparing early — about six months before the target start date.

What we worked on:
- **Separation practice** — short separations from primary caregivers, building to longer ones
- **Group settings** — library story time, music classes, situations with other kids and another adult in charge
- **Following group instructions** — not just my voice, but any adult's voice
- **Self-regulation vocabulary** — "big feelings," "calm body," "take a breath"

Questions to ask when touring preschools:
- What's your experience with sensory-sensitive children?
- What does your transition routine look like?
- How do you handle meltdowns?
- Is there a quiet space available?
- What's your communication with parents like?

We ended up with a small program — 8 kids, two teachers, lots of sensory play built into the curriculum. The size mattered. A classroom of 20 would have been overwhelming.

He started at 3 years and 2 months. The first two weeks were hard. By week four, he was running in.
