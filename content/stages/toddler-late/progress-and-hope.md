I started writing this journal when he was 4 days old, in a NICU chair with bad coffee and no sleep, because I needed to put words somewhere.

Now he's 30 months old. He tells knock knock jokes. He has a best friend at daycare. He knows all his shapes and most of the alphabet. He still has hard days. He still struggles with transitions. But the hard days are fewer and shorter.

This is not a redemption story where NAS turns out to be nothing. It's been real and hard and expensive in time and money and worry.

But this is also a story of a kid who is so much more than his diagnosis. He is funny and fierce and empathetic in ways that regularly astonish me. He notices when I'm sad. He brought me a toy dog when I was crying once and patted my knee.

The research on NAS outcomes is mixed — there are risk factors I can't ignore. But the research on protective factors is also clear: stable, nurturing caregiving is the single most powerful intervention. You showing up, every day, is the treatment.

What I want you to know, wherever you are in this journey:

The NICU doesn't last forever. The sleepless nights end. The meltdowns get shorter. The interventions work.

You are already doing the most important thing.
