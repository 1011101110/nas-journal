The feeding difficulty that started in the NICU didn't entirely resolve — it transformed. By 18 months, we'd graduated from "will he eat anything" to "will he eat anything that isn't beige and smooth."

Sensory processing issues often extend to food. Texture, temperature, smell — what registers as mildly interesting to a typical toddler can be genuinely aversive to a NAS toddler with sensory differences.

Our feeding therapist (yes, we have a lot of therapists) gave us a framework: **food chaining**. You start with what they eat, then make micro-changes in the direction of what you want them to eat. Similar color, similar texture, same shape, new ingredient. Very slow. Very patient.

Foods he ate reliably at 18 months:
- Dry cereal (Cheerios, Puffs)
- Yogurt (specific brand only)
- Banana (but only when perfectly ripe)
- Applesauce pouches
- Chicken nuggets (specific shape from specific brand)

We didn't fight it. We offered variety without pressure, celebrated any new food, and kept working with the feeding therapist.

The table approach that helped: **one safe food always present.** At every meal, something he reliably eats. So he never goes hungry, and there's no power struggle about eating.

This too has improved. At 2.5, the food repertoire is broader. Not typical, but enough.
