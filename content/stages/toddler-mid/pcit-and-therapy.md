Around 20 months, our developmental pediatrician mentioned PCIT — Parent-Child Interaction Therapy. I was resistant. "Therapy" implied something was broken. Nothing was broken.

She reframed it: "It's coaching for a high-intensity kid. Think of it as learning a specialized parenting language."

That landed. We found a PCIT therapist and started sessions.

PCIT has two phases: Child-Directed Interaction (CDI) and Parent-Directed Interaction (PDI). In CDI, you learn to follow the child's lead — specific skills with the acronym PRIDE: Praise, Reflection, Imitation, Description, Enthusiasm. No questions. No commands. No criticism. Just narrating and following.

It felt weird at first. "You're putting the blue block on top! Now the red one. You're building a tower!"

But something shifted. He started trusting that I would follow him. And from that foundation of trust, the PDI phase — where you introduce clear, consistent expectations — worked.

What PCIT changed:
- His meltdowns decreased in frequency by maybe 60%
- Recovery time shortened dramatically
- Our relationship felt different — less like managing a difficult child, more like knowing each other

PCIT is evidence-based specifically for ages 2-7. Look for therapists certified through PCIT International. Insurance often covers it.
