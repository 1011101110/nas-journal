title: "Transitions and Managing Change"
description: "Why NAS kids struggle with transitions and the tools that help."
stage: toddler-mid
order: 3
---

Moving from one activity to another — this is the trigger I underestimated for the longest time.

Every transition is a loss for a toddler. You're asking them to stop something they're engaged in and shift to something unknown. For NAS kids, the uncertainty of what comes next is particularly dysregulating.

The tools we use:

**Time warnings.** "Five more minutes, then we're leaving." "Two more minutes." "One more minute." This isn't about counting — it's about giving his nervous system time to prepare.

**Visual schedules.** Pictures of what comes next. We started with a simple card: park picture, car picture, home picture. Being able to see the sequence helped him understand that the park wasn't disappearing — we'd be back.

**Transition objects.** Bringing something from the current activity to the next one. A small toy from home that comes to the store. A comfort item that bridges the space.

**Narrating the transition.** "We're leaving the park now. I know that's hard. We're going to get in the car and go home. When we get home, we'll have lunch." The narration doesn't stop the meltdown, but it demonstrates that you understand, and over time that understanding becomes trust.

**Consistent sequences.** The morning routine is the same every day. Breakfast, then dress, then shoes, then bag. Same order. Every day.

These aren't accommodation — they're support. There's a difference.
