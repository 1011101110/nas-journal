## Screaming & High-Pitched Crying

**The short version:** NAS screaming — especially in infancy — is a nervous system event, not a behavioral one. The pitch and inconsolability are neurological. Management is about regulating the system, not stopping the behavior.

---

### Why This Happens in NAS Kids

The high-pitched, continuous cry of NAS is documented from the first days of life. It's distinct from typical infant crying — higher frequency, more persistent, less responsive to normal soothing.

The mechanism is direct: prenatal opioid exposure disrupts the developing autonomic nervous system, including the vagal tone that governs the transition between stress activation and calm. infant with POEs have:

- Higher baseline arousal (harder to settle into calm)
- Lower vagal tone (slower parasympathetic recovery after stress)
- Disrupted GABA signaling (GABA is the nervous system's brake — less GABA = harder to stop)
- Hypersensitivity to sensory input (light, sound, touch can all trigger screaming)

In older toddlers, the same mechanism drives screaming in meltdowns — the nervous system has escalated past its threshold and cannot self-regulate back down.

---

### Immediate Response

**⚡ For infants:**

1. **Reduce sensory input.** Dim the lights. Reduce sound. Swaddle firmly.
2. **White noise.** Consistent, loud-ish white noise (not music) is regulating for many infant with POEs. Brown noise or low-frequency static.
3. **Rhythmic movement.** Consistent rocking, swinging, or bouncing — same rhythm, not varied.
4. **Skin-to-skin.** Where tolerated (some infant with POEs are touch-sensitive), skin contact plus heartbeat rhythm.
5. **Don't panic.** Your escalating distress will escalate theirs. This is the hardest part. ([Co-Regulation](/interventions/co-regulation))
6. **Tag a co-caregiver.** If the screaming continues past 20–30 minutes, hand off. Caregiver dysregulation doesn't help.

**⚡ For toddlers in a screaming meltdown:**

1. Regulate yourself first (slow breath, slow movement).
2. Get to their level — crouch or sit.
3. Firm, calm physical presence. Don't try to talk them down mid-scream.
4. Minimal words: "I'm here. This is hard."
5. Firm deep pressure if they'll accept it.
6. Wait for the peak to pass before any talking.

### Structural Approaches

🏗️ **What to build into daily life:**

**Identify the triggers.** Keep a simple log for 1 week: what time did screaming occur, what preceded it (hunger, fatigue, stimulation, transition), how long did it last. Patterns emerge. Most POE kids have 2–3 reliable triggers.

**Reduce environmental load.** Noisy, busy, bright environments raise the baseline. Reduce unnecessary sensory complexity in your home environment.

**Predictable schedule.** Hunger and fatigue are the most reliable amplifiers of screaming episodes. Consistent meal and nap timing keeps both from hitting at high cortisol moments.

**PCIT CDI practice.** Regular undivided positive attention builds the child's capacity to use the parent-child relationship for regulation. Screaming episodes shorten over months of consistent CDI practice.

### Interventions That Help Here

🔬 **Long Game:**

- **[Co-Regulation](/interventions/co-regulation)** — Your regulated state is the primary immediate tool. Especially critical for screaming, where your stress response to the sound is involuntary.
- **[Routines & Predictability](/interventions/routines-predictability)** — Prevents hunger/fatigue triggers; reduces baseline cortisol.
- **[PCIT](/interventions/pcit)** — Long-game reduction in screaming frequency through relationship-based regulation capacity.

---

### What Worked For Us ★

For infants: the Marpac Dohm white noise machine. Analog sound, consistent tone, no algorithm variation. Better than apps or smart speakers for regulatory purposes.

For toddlers in screaming meltdowns: earplugs. Not ignoring the child — being present and regulated while protecting your own ears. Parental hearing strain causes real sympathetic nervous system activation that your child reads. Small thing, meaningful difference.
