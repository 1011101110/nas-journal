## ⚡ Try This Right Now

If you're seeing early overstimulation signs:
1. **Exit the environment if possible** — get to a calmer space now, before escalation
2. **Stop all demands** — no questions, no instructions, no expectations
3. **Reduce input** — quiet, dim, still
4. **Offer regulation** — deep pressure hug, heavy blanket, or just proximity
5. **Give time** — 10-15 minutes in a calm environment before re-engaging

## The Window Before the Meltdown

There is almost always a window. A period between "functioning well" and "fully melted down" where the nervous system is moving toward overload but hasn't hit the edge yet.

In this window, intervention is possible. After the edge, you're managing the storm, not preventing it.

Learning your child's early warning signs is one of the most valuable skills in NAS parenting.

## Common Early Warning Signs

**Physical:**
- Ears turning red
- Increased body movement (wiggling, rocking, running in circles)
- Changes in facial expression (glazed look, furrowed brow)
- Eye rubbing
- Covering ears

**Behavioral:**
- Increased silliness or hyperactivity (sometimes precedes crash)
- Clinginess
- Starting to get "stuck" on things
- Reduced ability to follow directions
- Picking at clothes, pulling at self

**Vocal:**
- Whining increases
- Pitch of voice rises
- Requests become more insistent

Every child's pattern is unique. Watch, document, learn their specific sequence.

## Building in Regulation Throughout the Day

NAS kids often need sensory regulation built into their day, not just reactive responses:

**Morning:** Heavy work before demands (carrying a backpack, pushing a laundry basket)
**Mid-morning:** Movement break
**After school/daycare:** Quiet decompression time before activities
**Pre-bedtime:** Calming routine (no screens, low stimulation)

Occupational therapists call this a "sensory diet" — a schedule of activities tailored to the child's sensory profile. Consider requesting an OT evaluation if sensory issues are significantly affecting daily function.
