## ⚡ Try This Right Now

If you think sensory overload is happening:
1. **Get to a quieter space immediately**
2. **Reduce visual input** — dim lights or find low-stimulation environment
3. **Lower your own voice** to near whisper
4. **Don't add more input** — stop talking, stop asking questions
5. **Offer proprioceptive input** — firm hug, deep pressure, weighted lap pad
6. **Wait** — let their nervous system recalibrate before re-engaging

## What Is Sensory Overload?

For NAS kids, the nervous system can become overwhelmed when sensory input exceeds its processing capacity. This is different from a tantrum (frustrated by not getting what they want) — this is the circuit breaker tripping.

Signs of sensory overload:
- Covering ears
- Meltdown that seems disproportionate to the trigger
- "Glazed" look or dissociation
- Vomiting (extreme cases)
- Aggression that seems to come from nowhere
- Crying that sounds different — more frantic, more desperate

## The Sensory Environment

NAS kids often have lower sensory thresholds in some areas and seek input in others. Common patterns:

**Auditory:** Sensitive to unexpected sounds, loud environments, multiple simultaneous sound sources

**Tactile:** May dislike light touch but tolerate or seek deep pressure; texture sensitivities with food and clothing

**Visual:** Can become overwhelmed in visually busy environments (busy patterns, lots of movement)

**Proprioceptive:** Often regulated by deep pressure, jumping, heavy work activities

## Environmental Modifications

- **At home:** Identify the quiet room. Keep it consistently low-stimulation. This is the go-to when things get too big.
- **At stores:** Go during non-peak hours. Have a plan for exit. Keep trips short.
- **At family events:** Create a quiet retreat space. Pre-brief family on the plan.
- **At school:** IEP/504 accommodations can include sensory breaks, preferential seating, access to fidgets

An occupational therapist specializing in sensory processing can do a formal evaluation and create a "sensory diet" — a schedule of sensory activities throughout the day that help regulate the nervous system.
