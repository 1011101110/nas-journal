## ⚡ Try This Right Now

If your NAS baby is not sleeping:
1. **White noise — louder than you think** (match ambient sound level, around 65-70 dB is safe)
2. **Swaddle tightly** if still in swaddling age (0-4 months)
3. **Dark room** — cover windows if needed
4. **Temperature check** — NAS babies run hot and sweat; 68-70°F room
5. **Consistent routine** — same sequence every time (bath → lotion → pajamas → feed → sleep)
6. **Put down drowsy** — not fully asleep, not fully awake

## Why NAS Babies Don't Sleep Like Other Babies

The Moro startle reflex that typically fades around 3-4 months often persists longer in NAS babies. Any sensory change — a sound, a shift in light, an air current — triggers the startle, which jolts them awake.

Additionally, NAS babies have a more alert nervous system at baseline. They don't drop into deep sleep as easily as typical babies. The sleep architecture is different.

This is not a behavioral problem. Sleep training methods designed for typical babies often don't work and can increase distress in NAS babies.

## What Worked For Us (Progression)

**0-3 months:** Contact naps, tight swaddle, very loud white noise, side-lying position (supervised), skin-to-skin. Survival mode.

**3-6 months:** Transition to swaddle alternatives (arms-up position, then one arm out, then both). Continue white noise. Start putting down in crib for at least some sleeps.

**6-12 months:** Consistent bedtime routine becomes possible and powerful. Same time, same sequence. Sleep sack replaces swaddle. White noise continues.

**12-24 months:** Routine solidifies. Can begin extending sleep independently. Continue white noise. Weighted sleep sack can help some kids.

**2+ years:** Most kids are sleeping reasonably by now, with some exceptions around transitions and developmental leaps.

## The Routine That Works

Our bedtime routine (30 minutes, every night, no exceptions):
- Warm bath (same temperature)
- Lotion massage (deep pressure, not light touch)
- Pajamas and sleep sack
- One book with dim light
- Nursing/bottle if age-appropriate
- White noise on
- Put down drowsy
- Leave with consistent phrase: "Goodnight. I love you. I'll see you in the morning."

The consistency is the therapy.
